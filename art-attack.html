CIRS / Art Attack

Made by Hand.

Painting, drawing, craft and the things made for the school’s stage.

Explore the work

The artwork wall

On the Wall.

348 works by CIRS students. 343 of them were printed in the Creative Corner of The Crossroads, the school’s magazine, across 32 issues; five come from the school’s archive. Each carries the credit it was printed with.

From the school’s archive, with the captions it gave them

The whole collection

Names and classes are given as the magazine printed them, and the class is the one the student was in at the time. Where the magazine printed no name, none is given here.

Inside the art room

How the Work Gets Made.

Art here is not an afternoon apart from the rest of school life. It is on the floor of the art room with a banner half-painted, on the tables of an exhibition being read by the students who made it, and in the masks and sets that end up under the stage lights.

01

Painting & drawing

The largest pieces are made on the floor, where there is room for them: banners, backdrops and collaborative paintings worked on by several students at once.

02

Craft & handwork

Craft is taught alongside painting — work made by hand, finished with care, and put out on the table for the school to see.

03

Made together

Some work is too large for one pair of hands. A banner is laid out along a table and worked on from both sides; a clay pot passes between several students, each taking a band of it.

One pot, several hands

The same pot, a few moments apart.

Two frames from the same sitting, painting pots for Pongal in January 2025: one pot, and several pairs of hands working around it, taking the brush in turn.

A house activity

Wall Magazines.

An annual artistic highlight at CIRS, the Wall Magazines bring each house together around the students who make them. Vivid colours, striking art, intricate detailing and bold quotes only begin to describe their place in school life.

Made for the stage

From the Art Room to the Stage.

Some of the art room’s work is made to be performed: masks, props and pieces of set that leave the studio for the school’s stage. The students here are holding up masks they made.

We have not yet matched them to photographs of the same masks in performance, so they are shown here on their own, with the people who made them.

The exhibition

Work on Display.

Finished work is put up for the school to see, and the students who made it stand beside it to talk visitors through it. Where the school’s records name the exhibition and its date, the caption does too.

Is one of these yours?

Many works here carry only a first name and an initial, and none has a title or medium, because the magazine printed none. The exhibition photographs are waiting for their exhibition names and dates. If you can supply a full name, a title or a date, write to info@cirschool.org and name the issue the work appeared in.
