The joy of little things,

The joy of little things,
From the sound of the bat to hearing the birds sing

From unnecessary sorrows, you must refrain.

Poem 04 of 16Opening line

One late noon, it was cloud’s day,

By Arnav Saraf · Grade 11 · Management

One late noon, it was cloud’s day,
The sky dark and the feeling gay.
Few soldiers fight in the battlefield,
While the king plays with no guilt.

I look at them and tell my lads,
“You are living your lives”.
Wish we’d be out of this mess,
To relive these times with complete bliss.

On a nearby puddle they play,
With their rides and swords they prey.
Slaying weach other and their guards,
And splashing the elixir on our lords.

The battle continues with screams
But for us, slaves, we only live with dreams.
The joy of these little things that we see at night
Are what we wish to attain with all our might.

Poem 14 of 16Opening line

The joy of little things

By Anushya UK · IB year 1

The joy of little things
is not waking up happy every day,
but finding small reasons to smile anyway.

Like getting an extra chocolate at the bottom of the bag,
or when your favorite song plays
right when you needed it.

It’s laughing over dumb jokes
that don’t even make sense later,
and sitting quietly with someone
enjoying the silence.

It’s the smell of rain,
the feeling when warm sunlight peeps in through the window,
and watching the same old movies
because it somehow feels comfortable.

We keep thinking joy has to be big,
like something special or planned,
but most of the time
it’s just hidden in simple moments.

And maybe that’s why it matters—
because the little things,
don’t try to impress you.
They just show up.

Poem 01 of 09Opening line

As the lonely boy lies curled against the wall,

By Tanmay Mehul Khakharia · IB year 2

As the lonely boy lies curled against the wall,
Hoping for a respite from the pain,
The thought that keeps him going, a light in the suffocating dark:
The freedom of turning a new page.

Compelled to give through and escape the despair,
The thought that drives his pen across the page:
The fulfillment of turning a new page.

As the woman crosses the war-ravaged fields,
Reaching for the safety beyond the booming weapons of hell,
The thought that pushes her broken, bloodied legs:
The hope of turning a new page.

from bouts of glory to moments engraved in memory
the constant cries of the sinners so incessant
plaguing the youth ever so effervescent
we are shrouded in the darkness of ignorance
owing to humanity’s unholy penance
the poisonous ego of the individual

forms a viciousness so habitual

alas! a doom may indeed be prophesized

the depths reached through the purity of a child
which can rid us of our profanity
it’s up to the children of today
to turn to pages of tomorrow
turning a new page to humanity
rooted in values of child-like purity

Poem 03 of 09Opening line

I turned a page today

By Ananda Priyan · Grade 12 · Science

I turned a page today
or something like it.
The corner folded clean,
no trace of what it held.
The words behave themselves now,
sit neatly in their lines.
No sudden slips of meaning,
no ink that runs too far.
Still, sometimes in the margin,
there’s pressure without a mark
as if a thought once rested there
and chose not to remain.
It’s nothing you could point to,
nothing you could prove.
Just how the paper softens
where it’s been held before.
I read it like it’s ordinary.
I let the sentences pass.
But something in the silence
lingers just a little longer.
I close the book or so I say,
But some pages don’t turn away.

Poem 04 of 09Opening line

Life, a tapestry of stories intertwined.

By Jishnu Teja Yogesh · IB year 1

Life, a tapestry of stories intertwined.
Some common yet some of a whole different kind.
A journal of pages, worn and torn.
The author, a child, from when they were born.

A tale that follows from cradle to grave.
Invincible, are those that end brave.
For trials will linger all along
And pull you away from where you strive to belong.

Yet fingers linger on pages once known
Tracing ink like it’s carved on stone.
But something within starts quietly to shift,
A tremor of change, a hesitant drift.
The ink of the past begins to release,
Its grip grows lighter, its echoes find peace.
The weight of “what was” no longer remains,
Just fragments of stories and softened refrains.
A silence appears—untouched, undefined,
No echoes behind, no script pre-designed.
And there, in the hush where nothing has stayed
You become the hand… and the unwritten blade.
