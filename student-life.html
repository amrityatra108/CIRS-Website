Student life

Live, Learn, Belong.

The boarding houses, the shape of an ordinary day, and the campus the whole of it happens on.

Live, learn, belong

A Campus That Never Really Closes.

Houses carry the names of India’s great teachers. Seniors and juniors share corridors by design, prep ends at nine, and the closing chant carries across the valley for six hundred boarders at once.

See a full day at CIRS

The daily routine

Sixteen hours, lived together.

Grades V to VIII and Grades IX to XII keep the same shape of day and run it about an hour apart. Here is each of them, hour by hour — scroll either one sideways to walk through it.

Academics are CBSE from Grade V and the IB Diploma from Grade XI, taught in English by CBSE subject teachers and IB-trained specialists. The four o’clock hour is athletics, football, cricket, basketball, swimming and yoga — eighty of the hundred acres are left wild, so the boundary of play is the edge of the Ghats. And the day closes the way it opens: six hundred students from twenty countries, one chant carrying across the valley.

The campus

A plan drawn around the trees.

Classrooms, houses, playing fields and the dining hall sit inside one walkable boundary. The rest of the site was left alone.

Academic Block

Laboratories, seminar rooms and both boards’ classrooms in one building.

Library

Reference, reading and the university-counselling desk together.

Dormitories

Residential houses named for the great sages of India.

Sports

Athletics track, courts, swimming pool and the yoga shala.

Amphitheatre

Built into the slope, with the valley as the back wall of the stage.

Dining Hall

A vegetarian kitchen serving the whole residential community.
