Final examination, May 2026

Twenty-four Candidates, Twenty-four Diplomas.

All twenty-four candidates registered for the International Baccalaureate Diploma in the May 2026 examination were awarded the diploma. The average total was 39.4 points, and the highest score was a perfect 45 out of 45.

Twelve students scored 40 points or above, eight scored between 36 and 39, and four scored below 36. Adithyan Diwakar Vidya and Shriya Shruti Misra were the toppers, both with 45 out of 45.

English & Arts

4–9 August 2025

English Week

The English Week Celebration filled the portals of CIRS with creativity, confidence and the joy of learning English. The array of competitions included Storytelling, Handwriting, Poetry Recitation, Spell Bee, Quiz and HAM.

Teachers seamlessly integrated language enrichment activities into their regular English classes throughout the week, ensuring every student had an opportunity to engage, express and excel.

Science

25 July 2025

Science Expo

As part of Science Week, the Department of Sciences hosted its Science Expo, turning the campus into a centre of exploration and innovation — over 140 activities and models across Biology, Physics, Chemistry and Environmental Science.

Presentations ranged from life processes and principles of motion to chemical reactions and sustainability, brought to life through interactive models, charts, digital displays and experiments. Around 50 schools from in and around Coimbatore attended.

Mathematics

7–11 July 2025

Mathematics Week 2025

Middle school students took part in the Maths Run, solving challenges at eight interactive stations, while senior students showcased their skills in a quiz that tested critical thinking, riddles, error-spotting and rapid recall. Children in Shishuvatika explored mathematics through traditional Indian games.

In the evening, 160 senior students competed in the house-wise Maths Run. The celebrations, aligned with the Chinmaya Vision Programme, strengthened logical reasoning.

Civic Life

25 April 2025

A classroom becomes a constituency

CIRS conducted its annual Class Representative Elections, giving students a hands-on experience of democracy. Each classroom functioned as a constituency, with votes cast through a structured process explained earlier in a Social Science assembly.

NCC cadets organised election kits and managed ballot collection, while class and assistant teachers oversaw proceedings from nominations to counting. Campaigns featured confident speeches and creative posters.

Service

From 19 April 2025

Seva Week

Every year, in the month of April, the students engage in selfless service as part of Seva Week. They organised awareness rallies, visited old age homes and rehabilitation centres, cleaned temples around the campus and decorated them with rangolis.

These seva activities instilled a sense of contributing positively to society, leaving the students with a profound feeling of fulfilment.

Festivals

April 2025

Vishu and Tamil Puthandu

The dorms came alive as students celebrated both Vishu and Tamil Puthandu, ushering in the new year with joy, culture and togetherness. Students viewed the Vishu Kani arrangement — the idol of Lord Vishnu, a mirror, fruits and vegetables, and the golden yellow Kanikonna flowers.

A traditional South Indian lunch was served on banana leaves, and the evening was given to cultural programmes.

Tradition

7 April 2025

Gayathri Havan

The academic year at CIRS commences with the Gayatri Havan, performed in the quadrangle of the Mathura Block. Students took part seeking the blessings of the Goddess Gayathri before beginning the new session.

The school describes it as a unique way of offering prayers to Surya Bhagwan, the Lord of the Brilliance of the Intellect.

Arts

Inter-house

Solo Instrument

The inter-house senior and junior dance competition was an eventful evening filled with joy. Many children lit up the stage in vivid costumes — Krishna, Hanuman, and even Narayan. Each movement and step filled the hall with a sense of divinity and purity.

The concept followed an open theme, and the students performed to classical and semi-classical tunes that were not adapted from films.
