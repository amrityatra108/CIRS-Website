The Five O’Clock Shift

AT FIVE, THE CAMPUS CHANGES.

When the afternoon study bell rings, residential life shifts from the silence of laboratories and classrooms into open air, sweat, and collective motion.

16:00 / 17:00 CLASSES END Books close. Juniors at 4:00, seniors at 5:00.
17:15 TEAMS FORM House kits on. Boots laced. Courts fill.
17:30 THE FIELD OPENS Under the Siruvani hills, every match begins.

House Competition

THE SCHOOL COMPETES AS ONE.

Four houses meet in basketball, cricket and football. The sports department also holds inter-house competitions in athletics, team events, chess, carrom and aquatics.

RED

Red house · The lion

  • From the school record: Vasishtha was the first house to pass a thousand points in the school’s history, and in February 2008 it described itself as the unbeaten champion of the march past. Its drills have used pompoms and poles, and before those, pyramids, umbrellas and leziums.

Explore Vasishtha House

YELLOW

Yellow house · The tiger

  • From the school record: Valmiki took both the overall championship and the best march past at Khel Mela 2008, and won the Senior Social Science Quiz in April that year. Its Khel Mela drill that season was a dupatta and cap drill.

Explore Valmiki House

GREEN

Green house · The eagle

  • From the school record: The eagle has been Vishwamitra’s symbol since 2005. The house won the Inter-House Aquatic Meet and the junior English quiz in the second term of 2008, and brought the fight drill — two armies, staged as a series of exercises — to Khel Mela for the first time.

Explore Vishwamitra House

BLUE

Blue house · The dragon

  • From the school record: Vyasa took the best drill cup at Khel Mela 2008 with a drill of hoops and a bamboo dance, and it won the Junior Science Quiz that November.

Explore Vyasa House

Student Perspectives

THE RESULT IS NEVER ONLY THE RESULT.

Sport teaches perseverance before it yields victory. Reflections on early mornings, consistency, and personal growth from CIRS student athletes.

The Crossroads · Issue 29 · February 2026

“You do not need talent to become successful. In fact, you need skills that do not take talent like punctuality, consistency and the spirit to outlast everyone else.”
Aarav Bhartia Class X B · Cricket & Distance Running

From waking at 4:30 AM for fitness drills to defending 20 underarm balls in basic academy practice, Aarav reflects on his three-year journey to becoming Man of the Match with a 5-wicket haul: “I observed that opportunities always came to me, as long as I remained sincere in my practice. Not only in sport but in every field.”

The Residential Rhythm

PERFORMANCE IS BUILT BEFORE THE MATCH.

Sport at CIRS does not exist in isolation. It is woven into a disciplined residential day where physical exertion balances academic concentration and spiritual practice.

05:30 AM

Rouser & Jogging

The campus stirs before sunrise. Junior houses rise at 4:50, seniors at 5:30, followed by morning jogging in the fresh air of the foothills and warm milk.

Grounding
08:10 AM – 13:30

Classrooms & Laboratories

After morning prayer and assembly, five hours of academic focus in CBSE and the IB Diploma. Mental rigor establishes the discipline carried into the afternoon.

Intellect
15:20 – 17:00

Study Period

Post-lunch rest gives way to quiet, self-directed preparation. Academic work is completed so students step onto the field with undivided attention.

Focus
16:00 / 17:00

The Field Opens

Ninety minutes of intensive physical movement: athletics on the track, swimming in the pool, racquet matches, and inter-house football and basketball.

The Arena
18:30 – 20:10

Prayer & Aarti

Recovery and transition. Evening refreshments are followed by shared prayers and aarti, settling the body and mind after physical competition.

Centering
22:00 PM

Lights Out

Dinner and evening study close at 22:00 (21:00 for juniors). Milk is served, houses settle, and the hundred-acre campus rests for tomorrow’s rouser.

THE DISCIPLINE STAYS.

Sport at CIRS is part of the education. It teaches preparation, responsibility, recovery and respect for the team. Long after the trophy is placed in the cabinet, the habit of showing up remains.
