The seventh school in India

The IB Diploma at CIRS.

CIRS was the seventh school in India authorised by the International Baccalaureate, Geneva. The Diploma runs over the final two years — Grades XI and XII — and is taken here as the alternative to the CBSE pathway the school has offered since Grade V.

The programme is built to be demanding in a particular way. A student takes six subjects rather than a stream, three or four of them at Higher Level, and carries all six to the end. The breadth is the point: a scientist keeps writing, a writer keeps up mathematics, and both keep a second language.

Around those six sit three requirements that belong to no single subject — an independent research essay, a course on the nature of knowledge itself, and a commitment to creativity, activity and service. They are what turn six subjects into a diploma.

Every IB teacher here is IB-trained, and several serve as examiners for the programme. University counselling runs alongside the two years through Naviance, beginning a year earlier in Grade X.

The shape of the two years

Six subject groups, three or four at Higher Level.

A student takes one subject from each group, so no one leaves a discipline behind at sixteen.

How the six are chosen

  • Six subjects One from each of Groups 1 to 5. The sixth is an arts subject from Group 6 — or, instead, a second subject from any of Groups 1 to 5.
  • Three or four at Higher Level At least three and no more than four are taken at Higher Level. The remaining subjects are taken at Standard Level.
  • All six to the end Every subject runs the full two years and is examined at the end of Grade XII. Nothing is dropped after the first year.

Higher and Standard Level

The two levels differ in how far a subject is taken rather than in how seriously it is treated. Both are examined; both count towards the Diploma.

What holds the six together

The core: an essay, a question and a commitment.

TOK

Theory of Knowledge

A course about how knowledge is made rather than about any one subject: what counts as evidence, how a claim is justified, and why disciplines disagree. Students examine and reflect on what they are being taught everywhere else.

EE

Extended Essay

An independent piece of research on a question the student chooses and frames, supervised but not directed. It is the first time most students carry a piece of academic writing from question to conclusion alone.

CAS

Creativity, Activity, Service

Learning that is not assessed by examination — something made, something done physically, and something given to people outside the school. On a residential campus this runs through the ordinary week rather than beside it.

How the Diploma is scored

Forty-five points, and how they are earned.

Every subject is marked on the same scale, set by the International Baccalaureate and moderated worldwide, so a CIRS score means what it means anywhere.

Internal and external assessment

Each subject is assessed in two ways. Internal assessment — laboratory investigations, oral work, portfolios, a mathematical exploration — is done over the two years and marked by the school’s teachers, then moderated externally by the IB. External assessment is the written examinations, taken at the end of Grade XII and marked by IB examiners around the world.

Marked against criteria, not a curve

IB assessment is criterion-related: a candidate is measured against published criteria drawn from each subject’s own aims, not against the other students sitting the same paper. A grade therefore carries the same meaning in Coimbatore as it does anywhere else, which is why the Diploma is read the way it is by universities. It is awarded on the whole programme rather than on any one subject.

How the work is done

Learning beyond the classroom.

The Diploma is assessed on what a student can do unaided, so the habits below are taught rather than assumed.

  • InquiryStarting from a question worth asking.
  • ResearchFinding sources, and weighing them.
  • Critical thinkingTesting a claim before accepting it.
  • CollaborationWork that depends on other people.
  • CommunicationWriting and speaking to be understood.
  • ReflectionReading back over one’s own thinking.
  • Independent learningCarrying work without being chased.
  • Problem solvingMethod when the answer is not given.

The IB Learner Profile

Ten attributes the programme aims at.

The Diploma is scored in points, but it is written around a description of the kind of learner it hopes to form. The same ten attributes run through every IB programme in the world, and they sit close to what the Chinmaya Vision Programme already asks of a student here.

  • InquirersCurious, and able to learn without being led.
  • KnowledgeableEngaging with ideas that matter across disciplines.
  • ThinkersReasoning critically to act on complex problems.
  • CommunicatorsExpressing themselves confidently in more than one language.
  • PrincipledActing with integrity, and owning the consequences.
  • Open-mindedValuing their own culture, and seeking out others.
  • CaringShowing empathy and compassion, and acting to serve.
  • Risk-takersMeeting the unfamiliar with forethought and resolve.
  • BalancedHolding the intellectual, physical and emotional together.
  • ReflectiveConsidering their own learning, and its limits.

A residential campus

Learning, living and growing together.

The Diploma asks for sustained independent work over two years, and a boarding school can give that a shape a day school cannot: study hours that are part of the day rather than negotiated at home, and teachers on the same campus when a piece of work stalls.

The rest of the week is not suspended for it. Houses, sport, the arts, clubs and the school’s service work continue through Grades XI and XII — and for a student in CAS they are not a distraction from the programme but part of it.

Value education runs across every grade here alongside the academic timetable, the senior years included.

After the Diploma

Preparing for university and beyond.

University counselling at CIRS runs through Naviance and begins in Grade X, a year before the Diploma starts.

Much of what an application asks for, the programme has already required: a research essay written and defended, coursework carried without supervision, a second language, and two years of evidence that the student can manage their own time.

The Extended Essay in particular is the piece students draw on most — it is the closest thing school offers to the work a first year at university asks for.
