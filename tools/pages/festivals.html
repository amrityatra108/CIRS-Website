Through the year

The School Comes to Life.

CIRS is a bustling place, with vibrant festivals adorning its pathways through the year — and the school comes to life with melodious paeans and energetic dances for every one of them.

The enthusiasm resonates in each heart long after the event is over, because of the surge of participation from the fifth grade to the twelfth.

These festivities are not limited to the student body. The faculty are the backbone of every festival, lending unwavering support to every last-minute arrangement.

01July–AugustA monsoon morning

Raksha Bandhan

Unfamiliar at first, students develop an unbreakable bond as brothers vow to protect their sisters. The programme begins with Krishnaji being tied the first rakhi. Siblings share their moments of love and mischief with the entire school during the morning pooja.

Then, while rakhis are being knotted, the school resonates with wishes and chocolate requests. As dusk falls, the brothers surprise their sisters with an emotional, relatable and heartfelt programme.

02August–SeptemberFrom morning into midnight

Janmashtami

The day begins with everyone seated in devotion for the pooja. A culmination of pure devotion and Kanha’s naughtiness radiates from every heart through the day, and students fast to express their bhakti towards the Supreme.

The midnight aarti and performance is packed with energy. Then comes the time to relive Krishna’s childhood, as each grade competes to be the first to break the matki.

03August–SeptemberFlowers, then the feast

Onam

Rooted in Kerala, Onam extends its roots all the way to CIRS, and is especially loved by every student — for its powerful symbol of sacrifice, and for the special feast, the sadhya.

After contenting hearts and stomachs, the spirited story of Mahabali, told through a skit, brings goosebumps to every spectator.

04August–SeptemberCloud on the hills

Ganesh Chaturthi

Mornings begin with the sacred worship of Vinayaka, and the joy of the prasad that follows. Evenings are filled with enthusiasm as every grade shows its devotion with renditions, dances and anecdotes for our dear Bappa.

At the core of the festival, the students handcraft their own murtis. As the season ends, everyone is on their toes, movements and voices in unison, as the procession carries the idol to be immersed in the school’s own lake.

05September–OctoberTorchlight after dusk

Dussehra

The day begins with the morning pooja, which brings a wave of calm. What truly brings the celebration to life are the mesmerising rangolis crafted by the students.

The school gathers to chant and witness the ultimate triumph of dharma — Ravan Dahan — and the evening sky is spellbound as the fireworks go up.

06JanuaryThe harvest sun

Pongal

Six hundred students, cooking in small groups.

One of the biggest harvest festivals, and the school is decorated in a majestic way. But the campus feels incomplete without the aroma of pongal swirling in the air, as six hundred students come together to prepare the delicacy in small groups.

Everyone goes around relishing the pongals prepared by the other teams — and then the winner of the most scrumptious pongal is declared.

Photographs of the cooking itself are still to be added.

07MarchColour on the field

Holi

The essence of the bonfire is brought to us through a skit in which Holika’s devious plans burn, and the countdown begins.

To CIRS, Holi brings a festive zeal of colours that soon find their owners. The Dronacharya field buzzes with excitement as colour paints every face.
