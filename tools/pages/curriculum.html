Academic journey

Learning changes shape as students grow.

Junior School covers Grades V to VIII; Senior School begins in Grade IX. From Grade XI, students choose between CBSE and the IB Diploma.

  1. V–VIII

    Junior School

    A broad beginning

    CBSE study begins in Grade V. Music, sport and the sciences are part of the wider Junior School experience.

  2. IX–X

    Senior School

    Towards the first board year

    CBSE continues through Grade X, when students sit the first of the school’s two CBSE board examinations.

  3. XI–XII

    Final two years

    A choice of pathway

    Students continue with CBSE or take the two-year IB Diploma Programme on the same residential campus.

CBSE

The national pathway, Grade V to XII.

CIRS is affiliated to the Central Board of Secondary Education, New Delhi. English is the medium of instruction.

X / XII

Two board milestones

Students sit CBSE board examinations in Grades X and XII. Senior CBSE study continues from Grade XI; families should confirm the current subjects and combinations directly with the school.

School confirmation needed

Class XI entrance routes have been described as Engineering, Medicine and Management. Confirm the streams currently available, subjects by grade and permitted combinations with the school.

IB Diploma Programme

A second path through the final two years.

CIRS is an IB World School. In Grades XI and XII, the Diploma Programme brings six subject groups together with its core: the Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge and Creativity, Activity, Service.

Students study three or four subjects at Higher Level and complete the Diploma core alongside their chosen subjects.

Learning beyond subjects

Knowledge, service and skill.

Academic study sits within the Chinmaya Vision Programme and the life of a residential school.

Morning assembly and prayer, yoga, value education, culture talks and service give that Vision a place in daily school life. Arts and sport also sit alongside classroom work.

Sequence requiring school confirmation

The school has previously described Bal Bhagavatam in the lower school and selected Bhagavad Geeta portions in later years. The current sequence by age group has not been confirmed.
