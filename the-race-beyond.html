The Race Beyond

Lokk Vishwavedaa

The Crossroads, Issue 29 · February 2026

It's Sunday. Millions are tuning into their digital screens to watch one thing. Fast cars. Actually, 20 of them, racing at speeds that vaporize everything in their path. Behind their wheels lies the brave racer, experiencing the rush. All for one cause. Pass the checkered flag first. For 2 hours, engines scream, tires burn, and the world witnesses pure acceleration. For most, F1 ends there. The Pinnacle of Speed, Motorsport.

A simple race. But wars are not fought in press meets and are not won in TV interviews. Beneath the glamor and the rush exists a system where the $$$ dictates performance, existence and survival. Three years ago F1 generated over $3.4 billion in revenue. Mind you that is a figure which can fund a small nation for 365 days. F1 is not just a mere motorsport, it is an economic machine.

Money enters F1 through channels that are specifically designed for control and predictability. The largest piece of this cake comes from the broadcast and the media rights. Companies are desperate over owning the rights to show F1. These rights are sold to various networks around the world, in safe, long term contracts. This is a failsafe that ensures no matter who the victor may be, the sport retains its money.

Governments too pay a staggeringly huge amount to host these races, with the figure being around 30 to 60 million dollars. This price is paid by the governments as F1 offers many perks with them. It offers visibility, prestige and strong geo-political branding. Add to this huge capital injections from corporate sponsorships, buying association with the team with innovation and dominance, these streams manage to create the constant ever flowing capital into the sport.

Beneath the roar of engines lies Formula One Management (FOM), the entity that truly commands the world of adrenaline. It owns the commercial rights, oversees and controls negotiations and easily decides who eats and who starves. It doesn't race, engineer or innovate, but this entity dictates the future of F1 motorsports. Its role is one of a Financial gatekeeper. The FOM collects tons of money and cleverly redistributes them selectively under the jurisdiction of the 'CONCORDE' agreement, while retaining its own benefits for its parent company, Liberty Media. This tells us that this is not competition. It is centralised, formalized, cold hard control. F1 exists as a monopoly.

The teams delve into their sector, rounding up to the crucial question. "How can I cross the checkered flag first?" injecting millions into aerodynamics, simulations and clean, data driven, statistic based marginal gains. The engineers responsible for this are absolute assets. The commanders of the engine, the drivers, are commodities. The logistics by itself amounts to millions, with the teams shifting across the continents all year round. The induction of the cost cap was cleverly marketed as reform, but in reality, it barely slowed down the spending. This reform was unable to touch the decades of accumulated advantages.

Outside the circuits, the F1 economy plunges into the national economies and the host countries observe massive boosts in their revenue due to tourism and active infrastructure betterment. The team owners reap their benefits. The payout system in F1 might seem unfair to most people, but in reality, the system is clear cut. The strongest and the fastest survive. The payments are based on the legacy of the wins and the team's current performance. The successful teams receive the "Legacy Bonus." The sponsors increasingly gravitate to the dominant and the proven teams. This creates a feedback loop. Money produces performance, the performance attracts the money, and restart.

While all of this is true, the public funds spent by the governments bear massive opportunity costs, which are ignored in this global race for recognition.

So what is F1 really about? Is it about speed? About skill? Is it even about the competition? F1 is a closed, deep and beautifully detailed economic environment where the money is injected through the media deals and public funds, formalised under the FOM, redistributed to protect its dominance, while reinvesting to maintain the positions, and exported as global influence. The racing isn't fake. But the outcome is often decided before the lights go silent. Money not only rewards success, it actively manufactures it.

So the next time you witness a system, don't stop at the surface-level details. Question it, observe it and then come up with your own thesis. The world is filled with puzzles to solve, and your mind is waiting for you to unlock them.

See you in the next issue, with yet another Fiscal Narrative!
