Identity

A new identity, deliberately chosen.

Deep purple, gold, dark red, black and white replace the previous navy-and-saffron scheme, requested to give the school a more distinctive, trend-forward mark. Purple carries the structure, gold is the primary accent on calls to action and labels, and maroon takes the secondary accent role — hover states, the dropcap, the IB tag — exactly where terracotta sat before.

Contrast

  • Ink on paper — #1A1119 on #FAF7F8, 17.3:1.
  • Secondary text — #4E4350 on paper, 8.8:1. Muted #7C7080 is reserved for labels and metadata, 4.4:1.
  • Gold on white is 2.4:1, so it never carries body text. It appears as a fill behind white/ink text (buttons), as a 2px rule, or as uppercase eyebrow text at 600 weight, where it passes as large text. On the dark purple grounds it climbs to 8:1, which is where it carries labels directly.
  • Focus is a 2px gold ring at 2px offset on every interactive element, including the SVG chart nodes.

The emblem

The real logo is in the build. It is the school's own emblem, taken from cirschool.org, with the white background knocked out to transparency so it sits cleanly on any ground. The source file is only 103×100px, which is enough for the 52px header lockup but not for print or a retina hero. The school should supply the vector master. Because the emblem's wordmark is dark, the header and footer lockups are both placed on light or controlled grounds.

Typography

The current site runs on Verdana, Tahoma and Arial, so there was no existing web typeface to preserve. Three families were chosen instead, each doing a job the others cannot.

Display — EB Garamond 400 / 500, roman and italic A school in the hills, and a hundred acres A Garamond, not a fashion serif. It carries true italics for emphasis (never bold), genuine small caps, and oldstyle figures. It is the register of a school founded by a Vedanta teacher, and it is the opposite of what a template reaches for.
Text — Schibsted Grotesk 400 / 500 / 600 Eighty of those acres are left to the forest. CBSE and the International Baccalaureate are taught under one faculty. A news grotesque with a sturdy, slightly quirky set. Deliberately not Inter. Carries navigation, running text, tables and data.
Devanagari — Tiro Devanagari Hindi ज्ञानं सेवा च कौशलम् The motto is set in Devanagari at full size as its own band, with the IAST transliteration and English gloss beneath. This is the single most distinctive element on the page, and it is distinctive because it is true to the school.

Typographic rules

  • Emphasis is italic, never bold, in any Garamond setting. Bold is reserved for the grotesque.
  • Small caps are real, drawn from the font via font-variant-caps: all-small-caps — not uppercase with letter-spacing faked on top.
  • Oldstyle figures in editorial settings (the timetable, the record figures, the fact rail), lining and tabular figures wherever numbers must align in a column.
  • One drop cap on the page, in maroon, opening the school's history. A second would make it a motif.

Four rules that keep it safe

  • Nothing is hidden in CSS. GSAP's from() sets the start state itself, so if the libraries are blocked the page simply renders complete.
  • A safety sweep runs on scroll, and it uses gsap.set(), not gsap.to(). The case it guards against is precisely the one where the ticker is not running, and a tween would never advance. It covers opacity-hidden elements and transform-hidden ones (masked lines and figures) alike.
  • The opening curtain has a hard 4.2-second ceiling on a setTimeout, which still fires in a background tab. A hidden tab suspends requestAnimationFrame, so a GSAP onComplete would never run — without the ceiling, a visitor who opened the page in a background tab could return to a full-screen panel with scroll locked. The intro is skipped outright if the document is hidden at load.
  • Sticky states read from three signals. Lenis suppresses the native scroll event, so a plain window.addEventListener("scroll", …) never fires while it is running — this silently broke the header shadow and the back-to-top button. They now listen via ScrollTrigger, an IntersectionObserver sentinel, and the native event, and act on whichever reports first.
  • prefers-reduced-motion skips Lenis, the curtain, the cursor ring, the ticker, the pin and every reveal. The horizontal day becomes a native swipe track, which is also what it becomes below 900px.

What changes

  • Navigation becomes a full-sitemap drawer below 1000px, showing every section at once rather than a nested accordion.
  • The utility bar keeps contact details and drops the accreditation line below 720px — a parent on a phone wants the phone number.
  • Hero type caps at 35px and the photograph shifts its focal point to keep the buildings in frame.
  • The chart keeps a 680px minimum drawing width inside its own horizontal scroll frame.
  • Tap targets are a minimum of 44×44px; hero buttons go full-width below 440px.
  • Images are lazily loaded below the fold and every one declares intrinsic width and height, so there is no layout shift.

Build plan

From prototype to a site the office can update.

The constraint that matters is not the design — it is that the school office must be able to publish an event, a date or a result without a developer.

LayerChoiceWhy
FrameworkAstroShips zero JavaScript for most of the site; only the chart needs hydrating. Content-site ergonomics without a single-page app.
ContentSanity or PayloadDay rows, life cards, facilities, admission steps and dates become structured entries the office edits directly.
MediaCloudinary or imgixAVIF and WebP with responsive srcset. Essential once real high-resolution photography arrives.
FormsExisting easycollege.inDo not rebuild the payment path. The site owns the journey and the visit booking, and hands off at step 01.

Performance budget

  • LCP under 2.5s on 4G. The hero headline is the LCP element and is server-rendered text, never an image.
  • Under 40KB of JavaScript. The current interaction layer is about 6KB unminified, with no framework and no animation library.
  • Two font families, five cuts, self-hosted and subset to Latin plus the diacritics used in the motto.
  • Zero layout shift — every image declares width and height.

Assets

What the school still has to supply.

The prototype uses only genuine CIRS material. Where something is missing, it is named here rather than faked.

  • Verification of the Devanagari motto. The band reads ज्ञानं सेवा च कौशलम् — reconstructed from the romanised form on the current site, since no Devanagari original was published. This must be checked by the school before launch; it is the most prominent single element on the page.
  • The emblem as vector. The build uses the real logo from cirschool.org with the white knocked out, but the source is 103×100px — enough for a 52px header lockup, not for print or retina.
  • A photography shoot. Every photograph is the school's own, re-cropped to remove the caption text baked into the originals and put through one colour grade — an S-curve with shadows pulled toward the purple and highlights toward cream — so that images shot over fifteen years on different cameras read as one commission. The highest-resolution source available publicly is 1400px wide after cropping; a modern site needs 2400px+ originals. The gallery images are all multi-photo collages with captions burnt in, so nothing usable as a standalone portrait could be cut from them.
  • Board results and university placements, signed off by the examinations office. The Academic Record section deliberately ships without percentages rather than with estimates.
  • The verified alumni destination register. The pathways chart plots thirteen sample cities from real coordinates; production should read from the register.
  • Student and alumni interviews, with consent, if a testimonial section is wanted. None is invented in this build.
  • Current fee structure and important dates for the open admission year.
  • Chinmaya Mission brand confirmation that this treatment of the emblem and motto is acceptable to the Central Chinmaya Mission Trust.
