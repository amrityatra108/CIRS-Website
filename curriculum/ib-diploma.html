Diploma at CIRS

Breadth, depth and work that is your own.

At CIRS, the IB Diploma Programme is an option for Grades XI and XII. It combines six subjects with a required core of research, reflection and activity.

Students choose from six subject groups, studying three or four subjects at Higher Level (HL) and the rest at Standard Level (SL). The two levels differ in scope, while both contribute to the Diploma score.

Alongside those subjects, every Diploma student completes Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay and Creativity, Activity, Service. Together they invite students to test ideas, pursue a question independently and take part in experiences beyond their courses.

The IB sets the programme framework. The subjects and levels available at CIRS for a particular intake need to be confirmed with the school.

Subject groups

Six groups keep the course broad.

The IB groups describe the shape of the Diploma, not a list of subjects currently offered by CIRS. An Arts subject may be replaced by an additional language, individuals and societies, or sciences subject.

  1. Group 1

    Studies in language and literature

    Read, interpret and write about language and texts.

  2. Group 2

    Language acquisition

    Develop understanding and communication in an additional language.

  3. Group 3

    Individuals and societies

    Investigate people, institutions and the world they shape.

  4. Group 4

    Sciences

    Examine the natural world through evidence and inquiry.

  5. Group 5

    Mathematics

    Study mathematical ideas, reasoning and applications.

  6. Group 6

    The arts

    Study an arts subject, or an additional permitted subject in languages, individuals and societies or sciences.

Ask for the current CIRS subject list.

Before planning a combination, ask the IB Coordinator which subjects CIRS offers for your entry year, which are available at HL or SL, and whether there are prerequisites or timetable limits.

Ask about subjects and levels Read the IB curriculum framework

The DP core

Three requirements connect the subjects.

The Diploma core is studied alongside the six subjects throughout the programme.

TOK

Theory of Knowledge

Students reflect on how knowledge is made and how we know what we claim to know.

EE

Extended Essay

Students pursue an independent research question and develop an extended piece of academic writing.

CAS

Creativity, Activity, Service

Students take part in experiences across all three strands. CAS is required for the Diploma but does not add points to the score.

Assessment

How the Diploma is assessed.

The six subjects and two scored core components contribute to a possible total of 45 points. The IB also sets minimum conditions for the award.

7
Points per subjectEach of the six subjects is graded from 1 to 7, contributing up to 42 points together.
+3
Possible core pointsTheory of Knowledge and the Extended Essay can contribute up to three further points.
45
Maximum totalCAS is required for the award of the Diploma but does not contribute points.

Coursework and examinations

Most courses include written examinations at the end of the Diploma. Depending on the subject, other work may be assessed externally or marked by teachers and moderated by the IB.

Award conditions

A candidate needs at least 24 points and must meet the IB’s other minimum requirements, including completion of the Diploma core. The current conditions are set by the IB.
