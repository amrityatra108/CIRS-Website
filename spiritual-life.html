IN THE VISION OF PUJYA GURUDEV

Every morning opens with the Bhagavad Gita and the Upanishads; every evening closes in bhajans. Between them runs a life of study, practice and service, in the vision of Pujya Gurudev Swami Chinmayananda.

The place of the spirit

The place of the spirit.

Pujya Gurudev Swami Chinmayananda envisioned an education that transforms rather than informs, and CIRS was built on that vision. The Chinmaya Vision Programme, which holds culture, philosophy and academics together, is not a subject on the timetable here. It shows up in the shape of the day.

Spirit is one of the four aspects the school sets out to educate, beside body, mind and intellect: the subtler aspects of personality, and the vast literature and scripture of Bharat. Alumni often recommend CIRS for the inner transformation students go through within its walls.

The daily rhythm

A Day Rooted in Awareness.

Four practices return every day, from the first class of the morning to the last light of the evening.

The full timetable, on The CIRS experience →

Every day

  1. Every morning

    Spiritual class स्वाध्याय

    The Bhagavad Gita, the Upanishads, and the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, studied as students’ own inheritance.

    About this practice

    Spiritual classes

    When
    Every morning
    Who
    Every student

    Every morning, students learn the sacred knowledge preserved in the Bhagavad Gita and the Upanishads.

    Alongside it they study the Ramayana and the Mahabharata as Bharat’s own rich history, and take inspiration from Bhagwan Shri Ram and Shri Krishna, embodiments of dharma. At a time when many young people are drawn away from their roots, values and culture, these classes give students a way back to them.

  2. Every morning

    Sādhanā, 108 times साधना

    Alone and in silence, each student chants a mantra of their choice 108 times.

    About this practice

    Daily sādhanā

    When
    Every morning
    Who
    Each student, alone
    Practice
    A mantra of their choice, 108 times

    Every morning students sit alone in silence and chant the mantra of their choice 108 times.

    The practice is known as “cleaning the clutter” stored in the mind. A mind full of distraction, always in the future or the past, slowly settles into the present moment, where reality is. Working from there, students meet the most efficient and productive version of themselves. It is one reason for the dynamism of CIRS students.

  3. Before every meal

    Prayer before food अन्न

    A pause to give thanks to Bhagwan for the anna on the plate.

    About this practice

    Prayer before food

    When
    Before every meal
    Who
    Every student

    Before every meal, students pause to give thanks to Bhagwan for the anna placed before them.

    In a land where agriculture has been at the heart of civilisation for thousands of years, anna is not merely food: it is revered. The prayer reminds students never to take their food, or the countless efforts behind it, for granted.

  4. Every evening

    Aarti and bhajans आरती

    After the long day, lively bhajans sung in devotion to Bhagwan.

    About this practice

    Aartis

    When
    Every evening
    Who
    Every student

    After a long and tiring day, students gather every evening to sing lively, energetic bhajans in devotion to Bhagwan.

    In that devotion, and in surrendering oneself to the Lord, the stress and negative tendencies of the day fall away. Students leave free and calm, with the energy to carry on.

I

स्वाध्याय

Swādhyāya · Study

The scriptures are read at CIRS as a living inheritance, and carried out to the places where the Mission’s own teachers are formed.

Educational and spiritual tours

When
Every year
Who
Every batch

Every year, every batch travels to a spiritually energised place as part of its educational tour.

Chinmaya Mission counts these places among its “Chaar Dhaam”. They matter, the school holds, because they are where living masters are formed, who go on to serve and touch the lives of many. Students call the tours transformational.

  1. 01

    Sidhbari Ashram

    The final resting place of Pujya Gurudev Swami Chinmayananda after his Mahasamadhi.

  2. 02

    Chinmaya International Foundation, Kerala

    At the birthplace of Adi Shankaracharya. Researchers from all over the world come here to study India’s scriptures and texts.

  3. 03

    Sandeepany Sadhanalaya, Mumbai

    The headquarters of Chinmaya Mission, where seekers from across the globe study the Vedanta course in English.

Sacred occasions

Practice kept daily, practice that trains the mind and the tongue, and the sacred occasions that mark the year.

Gayatri Havan गायत्री हवन
When
The start of each academic year
Who
Students

The new academic year begins with the Gayatri Mantra and an offering to the sacred fire.

As their studies resume, students purify the mind and invoke the divine energy of Mother Gayatri. The havan marks an auspicious new beginning: a chance to correct the previous year’s mistakes and to grow better, stronger and happier.

Festival pooja पूजा
When
The morning of every festival
Who
The whole school

Diwali, Holi, Ganesh Chaturthi: every festival at CIRS is kept with dynamism and devotion, and every one begins with a morning pooja invoking Bhagwan’s blessings on the whole school.

Each pooja is a surrender of the ego and a thanksgiving for all that has been given. It is a humbling experience: students come to see themselves as instruments through which Bhagwan serves the world.

The festivals of the school year →

Matru Pitru Pooja मातृ पितृ पूजा
Who
Students and their parents

For a parent, few moments are more moving than a child bowing before them in gratitude.

During this pooja, parents and children remember the countless moments they have shared since childhood. Students thank their parents for their love, sacrifices, guidance and care, and affirm that they will always respect, love and serve them.

Training the mind and the tongue

Medhā SūktamJapaSubhashi

Medhā Sūktam मेधासूक्तम्

When
Every morning in examination season; the nine days of Navratri
Practice
Chanted eleven times

When examinations bring days and nights of hard study, students chant the Medhā Sūktam, an offering to Mother Saraswati, eleven times every morning.

Given their whole attention, the chant leaves the mind calm and clear, and trains it to be completely focused on whatever it is doing. Concepts are grasped quickly and well. The Medhā Sūktam is also chanted through the nine days of Navratri, as an offering to Shakti.

Japa जप

When
Through the year
Practice
Around two hundred written pages a year

Over a year, a CIRS student writes around two hundred pages of japa.

Most often they write the name of the form of Bhagwan dearest to them, and some turn the name into artworks full of devotion. Like chanting, writing the name aligns body, mind and intellect, so that the work which follows is done well.

Subhashi सुभाषी

What
An award for pure, noble and righteous speech
Who
Students

Subhashi is an award for students whose speech stays pure, noble and righteous, free of negative and abusive language.

The aim is not merely to stop using bad words. It is to form the habit of speaking words that are truthful, respectful and uplifting, a habit that becomes lifelong and keeps a student pointed in the right direction.

III

सेवा

Sevā · Service

Service at CIRS is not a single act. It is taught as a lifelong commitment, and practised well beyond the campus.

Chinmaya Balsevak Program

What
120 hours of sevā in society
Who
Students
By
CCMT Education Cell (CCMTEC)

An initiative of CCMTEC in which students complete 120 hours of sevā, contributing to society, so that they understand service as a lifelong commitment rather than a one-time activity.

At CIRS students have led awareness rallies, cleaned temples, visited and spent time in orphanages, and made paper bags to distribute in government hospitals and schools.

Chinmaya Vishwa Sammelan

When
The forthcoming Sammelan
Who
CIRS students, as volunteers

At the forthcoming Chinmaya Vishwa Sammelan, CIRS students will serve Gurudev’s vision selflessly as volunteers.

They will run stalls and look after groups, and take on literary work, writing newsletters, magazines and articles for the Mahotsav. The work is a climb up the staircase of selflessness, and a training in leadership: learning to handle whatever situation arises.

Student spiritual development

Led by students.

In Chinmaya Yuva Kendra CIRS, and in the care seniors give juniors, students carry spiritual life for themselves and take it to others.

CHYK CIRS · FundraisingA fair of spiritual books

Chyks sell spiritual books, among them the Bhagavad Gita, Kindle Life and We Must, to spread their knowledge and inspire readers to improve their lives. The book fair has raised more than one lakh rupees for projects Chinmaya Mission takes up for the world.

CHYK CIRS · At Anand UtsavCHYK merchandise

At Anand Utsav, one of the biggest festivals at CIRS, CHYK CIRS sells T-shirts, hoodies, pens, stickers, bottles and notebooks designed to connect students with their nation and culture. The proceeds go to charity work.

CHYK CIRS · Every yearChyk Yuva Samvad

Speakers chosen from CIRS address students of other schools in Coimbatore on Bharatiya culture and history, awakening curiosity about the true nature of Bharat. The speakers grow as organisers and speakers, in the service of a higher purpose.

Seniors and juniorsMentorship

In the gurukul, teacher and student lived together, and the teacher guided each student by knowing them fully. CIRS, a residential school, keeps that spirit: senior students mentor juniors and inspire them to become leaders who contribute to society. Personal mentorship is among the most important parts of student life here.

ज्ञानं सेवा च कौशलम्

Jñānaṃ Sevā ca Kauśalam

Character, carried into every day.

None of this is kept apart from ordinary school life. It is heard in the way students speak, which the Subhashi award honours; seen in the pause before every meal and the evening’s bhajans; and carried outwards in a hundred and twenty hours of sevā and the work of the Yuva Kendra.

The school’s motto holds the whole of it: knowledge is gained to serve the world efficiently, with compassion and dedication.
